# Downtown Mobility Budget Brief

The draft budget shifts operating funds from peak-hour parking enforcement1<sup>[\[1\]](#footnote-1)</sup> to evening bus frequency pilots in the central corridor2<sup>[\[2\]](#footnote-2)</sup>, with the stated goal of reducing wait times before winter service changes take effect.

Analysts note that the projected ridership gain depends on temporary signal-priority equipment3<sup>[\[3\]](#footnote-3)</sup> and on extending the regional fare cap to municipal passes4<sup>[\[4\]](#footnote-4)</sup>, neither of which is fully funded in the current ordinance.

Council staff also warned that storefront loading zones may be reduced on two blocks near the market5<sup>[\[5\]](#footnote-5)</sup>, a tradeoff merchants accepted only after the city promised a six-month review6<sup>[\[6\]](#footnote-6)</sup>.

1. Parking enforcement redeployment is described in the March staffing memo approved by the transportation committee. [↑](#footnote-ref-1)

2. The pilot covers Route 6 and Route 14 between 6 p.m. and 10 p.m. on weekdays. [↑](#footnote-ref-2)

3. Temporary signal-priority units are leased for nine months and require separate maintenance funding. [↑](#footnote-ref-3)

4. The fare-cap extension is contingent on a data-sharing agreement with the regional transit authority. [↑](#footnote-ref-4)

5. The affected blocks are on Alder Street between 3rd and 5th Avenue. [↑](#footnote-ref-5)

6. The review period begins after installation and includes monthly delivery-time observations. [↑](#footnote-ref-6)
