# Downtown Mobility Budget Brief

The draft budget shifts operating funds from peak-hour parking enforcement1[^1] to evening bus frequency pilots in the central corridor2[^2], with the stated goal of reducing wait times before winter service changes take effect.

Analysts note that the projected ridership gain depends on temporary signal-priority equipment3[^3] and on extending the regional fare cap to municipal passes4[^4], neither of which is fully funded in the current ordinance.

Council staff also warned that storefront loading zones may be reduced on two blocks near the market5[^5], a tradeoff merchants accepted only after the city promised a six-month review6[^6].

[^1]: Parking enforcement redeployment is described in the March staffing memo approved by the transportation committee.

[^2]: The pilot covers Route 6 and Route 14 between 6 p.m. and 10 p.m. on weekdays.

[^3]: Temporary signal-priority units are leased for nine months and require separate maintenance funding.

[^4]: The fare-cap extension is contingent on a data-sharing agreement with the regional transit authority.

[^5]: The affected blocks are on Alder Street between 3rd and 5th Avenue.

[^6]: The review period begins after installation and includes monthly delivery-time observations.
