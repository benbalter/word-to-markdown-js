# City Budget Brief

The finance office reported that transit ridership recovered to 92% of pre-2020 levels[^1], while fuel costs for the municipal fleet rose by 14% year over year[^2].

Council also approved a reserve transfer of \$3.5 million[^3] to cover storm drain repairs identified after the spring audit[^4], but deferred sidewalk lighting upgrades until next quarter[^5].

[^1]: Ridership estimate is based on fare-gate counts from January through September.

[^2]: Fuel costs reflect blended diesel and gasoline purchasing contracts.

[^3]: The transfer comes from the capital stabilization reserve.

[^4]: The audit reviewed 118 drain segments across three districts.

[^5]: Lighting upgrades were postponed pending revised contractor bids.
