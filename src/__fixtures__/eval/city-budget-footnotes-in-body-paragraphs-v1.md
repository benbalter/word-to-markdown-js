# City Budget Brief

The finance office reported that transit ridership recovered to 92% of pre-2020 levels<sup>[\[1\]](#footnote-1)</sup>, while fuel costs for the municipal fleet rose by 14% year over year<sup>[\[2\]](#footnote-2)</sup>.

Council also approved a reserve transfer of \$3.5 million<sup>[\[3\]](#footnote-3)</sup> to cover storm drain repairs identified after the spring audit<sup>[\[4\]](#footnote-4)</sup>, but deferred sidewalk lighting upgrades until next quarter<sup>[\[5\]](#footnote-5)</sup>.

1. Ridership estimate is based on fare-gate counts from January through September. [↑](#footnote-ref-1)

2. Fuel costs reflect blended diesel and gasoline purchasing contracts. [↑](#footnote-ref-2)

3. The transfer comes from the capital stabilization reserve. [↑](#footnote-ref-3)

4. The audit reviewed 118 drain segments across three districts. [↑](#footnote-ref-4)

5. Lighting upgrades were postponed pending revised contractor bids. [↑](#footnote-ref-5)
